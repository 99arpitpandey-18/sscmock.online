1. Modals क्या होते हैं?

Modal verbs (सहायक क्रियाएँ) वे verbs हैं जो main verb के साथ आकर उसके mood (रूप) को दिखाते हैं – जैसे ability (क्षमता), possibility (संभावना), permission (अनुमति), obligation (बाध्यता) आदि।

⭐ सबसे पहले यह याद रखें

Modal verbs always come before the base form (V1) of the main verb.
They do not change form (no -s, -ed, -ing)।

Example:
I can swim. (correct)
I can swims. ❌
I can swam. ❌
I can swimming. ❌

2. List of Modal Verbs

Modal Main Uses

Could past ability, polite request, possibility
May permission, possibility, wish
Might weak possibility, suggestion
Shall future (with I/we), suggestion, offer
Should advice, obligation, expectation
Will future, willingness, prediction
Would past habit, polite request, conditional
Must strong obligation, certainty, necessity
Ought to moral duty, advice, expectation
Used to past habit / past state
Need necessity (negative/interrogative)
Dare courage (negative/interrogative)

3. Characteristics of Modals

⭐ Important SSC Rules
  • Modal + V1 (base form) – no 'to' (except 'ought to')
  • No -s in third person singular
    He can swim. (not: He cans swim.)
  • No -ed (past) or -ing
    She could go. (not: She could went.)
  • Negatives: Modal + not
    cannot, could not, may not, must not
  • Questions: Modal + subject + V1
    Can you help me?

4. CAN – Ability / Permission / Possibility

1. Ability (क्षमता)

I can speak three languages.

She can play the piano.

Can you lift this box?

2. Permission (अनुमति) – informal

Can I go to the park?

You can use my phone.

3. Possibility (संभावना)

It can rain today.

Anybody can make mistakes.

4. Request (अनुरोध)

Can you please help me?

5. COULD – Past Ability / Polite Request

1. Past Ability (भूतकाल की क्षमता)

I could run fast when I was young.

She could speak French before.

2. Polite Request (विनम्र अनुरोध)

Could you please open the window?

Could I borrow your book?

3. Possibility (संभावना – less certain than 'can')

It could rain tomorrow.

He could be at home now.

6. MAY – Permission / Possibility

1. Permission (अनुमति) – formal

May I come in, sir?

You may leave now.

2. Possibility (संभावना)

She may be at the office.

It may rain this evening.

3. Wish (इच्छा)

May you live long!

May God bless you!

7. MIGHT – Weak Possibility

Might = less certain than 'may'।
दूर की संभावना / weak possibility।

It might rain today. (less certain)

He might come to the party.

She might be sleeping now.

8. SHALL / SHOULD

SHALL – Future with I/We, Offer, Suggestion

Shall I open the window? (suggestion)

I shall meet you tomorrow. (future – I/We)

Shall we go for a walk? (suggestion)

Exam Alert

In modern English, 'will' is more common than 'shall' for future.
'Shall' is mainly used with I and We for suggestions and offers.

SHOULD – Advice, Obligation, Expectation

You should exercise daily. (advice)

He should be here by now. (expectation)

We should respect our elders. (moral obligation)

9. WILL / WOULD

WILL – Future, Willingness, Prediction

I will meet you tomorrow. (future)

I will help you. (willingness)

It will rain today. (prediction)

WOULD – Past Habit, Polite Request, Conditional

When I was young, I would play cricket. (past habit)

Would you like some tea? (polite offer)

If I were you, I would accept the offer. (conditional)

10. MUST – Strong Obligation / Certainty

1. Strong Obligation (प्रबल बाध्यता)

You must wear a helmet.

We must obey the laws.

2. Certainty (निश्चितता)

He must be sleeping. (I'm sure)

She must be at home.

3. Prohibition (निषेध) – Must not

You must not smoke here.

Students must not run in the corridor.

Exam Alert

Must vs Have to:
Must = internal obligation (I feel it)
Have to = external obligation (rules / others)

Mustn't vs Don't have to:
Mustn't = prohibition (निषेध)
Don't have to = not necessary (ज़रूरी नहीं)

11. OUGHT TO – Moral Duty / Advice

Ought to = 'should' के समान, लेकिन थोड़ा अधिक formal और moral duty पर ज़ोर देता है।

You ought to respect your parents.

He ought to apologize for his mistake.

She ought to study harder.

⭐ Important Rule

Ought to = modal + to + V1
(Only modal with 'to')

12. USED TO – Past Habit / Past State

Used to का प्रयोग past habits (भूतकाल की आदतों) और past states के लिए होता है।

I used to play cricket when I was a child. (habit)

She used to live in Delhi. (past state)

He used to smoke, but now he doesn't.

Exam Alert

'Used to' और 'Would' में अंतर:
Used to = past habits + past states (both)
Would = only past habits (not past states)

✅ I used to live in Mumbai. (state)
❌ I would live in Mumbai. (incorrect for state)

13. NEED / DARE – Semi-Modals

Need और Dare को semi-modals कहते हैं क्योंकि ये modal और main verb दोनों की तरह behave कर सकते हैं।

NEED – Necessity (ज़रूरत)

Need I go there? (modal – interrogative)

You need not worry. (modal – negative)

He needs help. (main verb)

DARE – Courage (हिम्मत)

Dare he speak to me? (modal – interrogative)

He dare not enter the room. (modal – negative)

He dares to speak the truth. (main verb)

⭐ SSC Rule

Need और Dare के साथ not लगने पर ये modal की तरह behave करते हैं और V1 लेते हैं।
Affirmative में ये main verb की तरह behave करते हैं।

15. Common Errors – SSC

Incorrect Correct Reason
He can swims. He can swim. Modal के बाद V1 (base form)
She could went. She could go. Modal के बाद V1
You must to go. You must go. 'Must' के साथ 'to' नहीं आता
You should to study. You should study. 'Should' के साथ 'to' नहीं आता
He can not come. He cannot come. 'Cannot' one word
You mustn't to go. You mustn't go. Modal + not + V1
I would used to play. I used to play. 'Would' और 'used to' एक साथ नहीं
May you help me? Can you help me? 'May' का प्रयोग permission के लिए, request के लिए 'can/could'

16. SSC Modals Tricks

🔥 Trick 1 – Modal + V1

Modal के बाद हमेशा V1 (base form) आता है।
✅ I can swim.
❌ I can swims.

🔥 Trick 2 – Can vs Could

Can = present ability
Could = past ability / polite

🔥 Trick 3 – May vs Might

May = more possibility (50-60%)
Might = less possibility (30-40%)

🔥 Trick 4 – Must vs Should

Must = strong obligation (must do)
Should = advice (good to do)

🔥 Trick 5 – Used to vs Would

Used to = past habits + past states
Would = only past habits

🔥 Trick 6 – Need / Dare

Negative/Interrogative → modal (need not / dare not)
Affirmative → main verb (needs to / dares to)

17. SSC PYQ-Based / Exam Pattern Practice

Question 1 SSC CGL 2022

Choose the correct modal:

When I was a child, I _____ play in the park every evening.

A. should
B. used to
C. must
D. may
Answer: B. used to

Past habit (childhood) → 'used to' सही है।

Question 2 SSC CHSL 2021

Choose the correct modal:

You _____ not worry about the exam. It's easy.

A. must
B. should
C. need
D. dare
Answer: C. need

'Need not' = ज़रूरत नहीं है। (no necessity)

Question 3 SSC CPO 2022

Choose the correct modal:

_____ I come in, sir?

A. Can
B. May
C. Will
D. Should
Answer: B. May

'May' formal permission के लिए (with sir)।

Question 4 SSC MTS 2022

Choose the correct modal:

She _____ speak three languages when she was five.

A. can
B. may
C. could
D. must
Answer: C. could

Past ability → 'could'।

Question 5 SSC CGL 2023

Choose the correct modal:

You _____ obey the traffic rules.

A. can
B. may
C. must
D. might
Answer: C. must

Strong obligation (laws/rules) → 'must'।

Question 6 SSC CHSL 2023

Choose the correct modal:

He _____ be at home. The lights are on.

A. can
B. must
C. may
D. should
Answer: B. must

Certainty (sure) – lights on → must be at home।

Question 7 SSC CPO 2023

Choose the correct modal:

_____ you like to have some coffee?

A. Can
B. Will
C. Would
D. Shall
Answer: C. Would

Polite offer → 'Would you like...'।

Question 8 SSC MTS 2023

Choose the correct modal:

You _____ apologize for your mistake.

A. ought to
B. can
C. might
D. will
Answer: A. ought to

Moral duty / advice → 'ought to' (should के समान)।

18. Quick Revision – Modals

Rule 2 No -s / -ed / -ing
Rule 3 Can = ability / permission
Rule 4 Could = past ability / polite
Rule 5 May = permission / possibility
Rule 6 Might = weak possibility
Rule 7 Should = advice / obligation
Rule 8 Must = strong obligation / certainty
Rule 9 Ought to = moral duty
Rule 10 Used to = past habit/state
🎯 SSC Modals Final Checklist
  • Modal + V1 (base form) – हमेशा याद रखें।
  • Can = present ability / informal permission।
  • Could = past ability / polite request।
  • May = formal permission / possibility / wish।
  • Might = weak possibility (less than may)।
  • Shall = future with I/We / suggestion / offer।
  • Should = advice / moral obligation / expectation।
  • Will = future / willingness / prediction।
  • Would = past habit / polite request / conditional।
  • Must = strong obligation / certainty / prohibition (must not)।
  • Ought to = moral duty (modal + to + V1)।
  • Used to = past habits + past states (not 'would' for states)।
  • Need / Dare = semi-modals (neg/interrogative → modal form)।
